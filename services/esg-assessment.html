ESG Assessment & Materiality

A structured review of your environmental, social and governance performance, and of which issues matter most to your stakeholders.

Who it is for

Organisations starting their ESG work, or responding to questions from lenders, investors, parent companies or regulators.

What is included

  • Document review and management interviews
  • Stakeholder mapping and materiality assessment
  • Gap analysis against the chosen framework
  • Prioritised list of risks and opportunities

What is not included

  • Independent assurance or audit opinions
  • Implementation of the recommended actions unless agreed separately

Method and steps

  1. Scope. Agree objectives, sites, standards and information needed.
  2. Collect. Review documents, interview staff and, where agreed, stakeholders.
  3. Analyse. Assess gaps and rate issues by importance and risk.
  4. Report. Present findings, review a draft with you, then issue the final report.

Standards and laws we work to

  • GRI Standards (materiality and topic standards)
  • IFRS S1 and S2 for investor-focused reporting
  • IFC Performance Standards where lenders require them

Orientation only. Confirm current requirements with the regulator or a qualified adviser.

Frequently asked questions

What is a materiality assessment?

It identifies the ESG issues that matter most to your business and your stakeholders, so effort goes where it counts.

Do we need an ESG assessment before reporting?

It is the usual first step: it shows what data you have, what is missing and what to report.

What you receive

  • Gap analysis report
  • Materiality matrix and short methodology note
  • Prioritised action list

Timeline and fees

Timeline and fee basis are set out in a written proposal after a short scoping conversation, because they depend on scope, site access and the standards required.
